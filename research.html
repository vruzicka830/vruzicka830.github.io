Summary of time in graduate school

From Fall 2020 to Spring 2026, I was a graduate student at the University of Wyoming in the Department of Mathematics & Statistics.

Due to the presence in the department of Zhuang Niu, one of the world's C*-algebra experts, I became interested in C*-algebras myself. Indeed, sometimes a person's enthusiasm for their work inspires others to pursue a similar goal.

Zhuang suggested that I take a look at the "generator problem". This problem asks about the single generation of C*-algebras. What does it mean for a C*-algebra to be singly generated? Consider the integers as an analogy. The integers are the set of all positive and negative (and zero) "whole" (i.e., non-fractional) numbers. The number 1 "generates" this set since any integer can be obtained by adding or subtracting 1 as many times as necessary. For example, to obtain the number 3, one adds the number 1 three times; to obtain the number -1,000,000, one subtracts the number 1 one million times.

Similarly, one says that a C*-algebra is singly generated if there exists a member of the algebra from which one can somehow obtain every other member of the algebra. With the help of Chung Guang Li (who visited Wyoming in the winter of 2023–24), Zhuang and I were able to show that the C*-algebras in a certain ill-behaved class are singly generated.

While this project on the generator problem was under way, Zhuang introduced me to his collaborator George Elliott. George and I went on to prove a theorem about a particular collection of functions on a certain class of C*-algebras. In particular, we showed that the this collection of functions has the odd geometric shape of something called the Poulsen simplex. One might imagine the Poulsen simplex to be a sort of infinite-dimensional polyhedron with infinitely many vertices.

Zhuang and George became the official supervisors for my dissertation, which was based on the results just described.
